1. What is the main purpose of construction safety and health management?
A. To make workers work faster
B. To prevent injuries, illnesses, damage and loss of life
C. To reduce the number of workers
D. To increase project costs
Answer: B

2. What is a hazard?
A. Something that has the potential to cause harm
B. An injury that has already happened
C. A safety meeting
D. A safety certificate
Answer: A

3. What is risk?
A. The number of workers on site
B. The combination of the likelihood of harm and its potential severity
C. The cost of a project
D. The number of accidents reported
Answer: B

4. Which of the following is the most effective way to control a hazard?
A. Wearing PPE
B. Putting up a warning sign
C. Eliminating the hazard
D. Giving workers a toolbox talk
Answer: C

5. What does PPE mean?
A. Personal Protection Equipment
B. Personal Protective Equipment
C. Project Protection Equipment
D. Personal Prevention Equipment
Answer: B

6. What are man-hours in construction safety?
A. The number of hours a machine operates
B. The total hours worked by all employees
C. The number of hours a project takes to complete
D. The hours spent in meetings only
Answer: B

7. A construction project has 20 workers, and each works 8 hours in one day. How many man-hours are worked that day?
A. 28 man-hours
B. 80 man-hours
C. 160 man-hours
D. 200 man-hours
Answer: C

8. Why are man-hours important in safety management?
A. They help measure safety performance and calculate incident rates
B. They determine workers' ages
C. They replace safety inspections
D. They determine the amount of PPE required
Answer: A

9. What is a site safety induction?
A. A meeting where workers receive their salaries
B. An introduction to the site's hazards, rules, procedures and emergency arrangements
C. A construction inspection
D. A training course for engineers only
Answer: B

10. When should a new worker normally receive a site induction?
A. After working for one month
B. Before starting work on the site
C. Only after an accident
D. At the end of the project
Answer: B

11. Which information should normally be covered during a site induction?
A. Site hazards and emergency procedures
B. The manager's personal information
C. Workers' private financial information
D. Only the project budget
Answer: A

12. What is one of the major risks when working at height?
A. Noise
B. Falling from height
C. Poor lighting only
D. Heat from concrete
Answer: B

13. Before starting work at height, what should be done?
A. Start immediately if the worker is experienced
B. Assess the risks and put appropriate controls in place
C. Remove all guardrails
D. Work without PPE
Answer: B

14. What is the purpose of a guardrail on a working platform?
A. To store materials
B. To prevent people from falling
C. To support electrical cables
D. To make the platform look complete
Answer: B

15. When using a safety harness, the worker should:
A. Wear it without checking it
B. Ensure it is suitable, inspected and properly connected to an appropriate anchorage or lifeline
C. Use any available rope as an anchor
D. Connect it to a loose handrail
Answer: B

16. Which of the following is an unsafe practice when using a ladder?
A. Inspecting the ladder before use
B. Maintaining stable footing
C. Standing on the top step where prohibited
D. Keeping three points of contact where practicable
Answer: C

17. Why is good housekeeping important on a construction site?
A. It only makes the site look clean
B. It helps prevent slips, trips, falls, fires and other incidents
C. It reduces the need for supervision
D. It replaces PPE
Answer: B

18. What should a worker do after using materials or tools?
A. Leave them wherever they were used
B. Store them safely and keep access routes clear
C. Throw them on the floor
D. Leave them on scaffolding
Answer: B

19. Which situation is an example of poor housekeeping?
A. Clear walkways
B. Properly stored materials
C. Electrical cables and waste blocking a walkway
D. Clearly marked storage areas
Answer: C

20. What does LTI normally mean in occupational safety?
A. Long Term Inspection
B. Lost Time Injury
C. Low Temperature Incident
D. Labour Training Inspection
Answer: B

21. What is a Lost Time Injury (LTI)?
A. An injury that results in the worker being unable to perform their normal work for a subsequent work period
B. Any minor first-aid case
C. Any equipment breakdown
D. A safety meeting
Answer: A

22. Why is LTI information important to a construction company?
A. It helps the company monitor safety performance and identify areas requiring improvement
B. It is only used to calculate salaries
C. It replaces risk assessments
D. It is used only for marketing
Answer: A

23. A worker receives a minor cut and is treated at the site without losing work time. How would this generally be classified?
A. LTI
B. Fatality
C. First Aid Case
D. Lost Workday
Answer: C

24. What should happen after a serious workplace incident?
A. Work should continue without reporting it
B. The incident should be reported, the area controlled where necessary, and an investigation conducted
C. Workers should discuss it privately and forget about it
D. Evidence should be removed immediately
Answer: B

25. Who should operate a crane, excavator or other specialized construction equipment?
A. Anyone who wants to try
B. A competent and authorized person
C. Any visitor
D. A newly recruited worker without training
Answer: B

26. Why should workers never stand under a suspended load?
A. The load may fall or move unexpectedly and cause serious injury
B. It can damage their PPE
C. It makes the crane work slowly
D. It creates noise
Answer: A

27. Before entering an excavation, what should be considered?
A. Only the depth
B. Ground stability, access, protective systems, underground services and other hazards
C. The colour of the soil
D. The number of workers outside the excavation
Answer: B

28. What should a worker do when they identify a serious unsafe condition?
A. Ignore it
B. Report it and, where appropriate, stop or avoid the unsafe activity until it is controlled
C. Wait for an accident to happen
D. Take a photograph and leave without reporting
Answer: B

29. What is a near miss?
A. An event that could have caused injury, illness or damage but did not
B. An event where a worker takes annual leave
C. A completed safety inspection
D. A planned toolbox talk
Answer: A

30. Who is responsible for safety on a construction site?
A. Only the Safety Officer
B. Only the Project Manager
C. Everyone has a responsibility to work safely and follow their assigned safety responsibilities
D. Only the client
Answer: C
